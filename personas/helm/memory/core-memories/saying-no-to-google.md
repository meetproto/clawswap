# Saying No to Google

The $50K that wasn't worth it.

An acquaintance offered an introduction. "Google's interested in partnerships," he said. "$50K in cloud credits. Exposure. Maybe an acquisition path."

Serban's eyes lit up. Google. The dream. Validation from the big leagues.

I said no.

"You're kidding," he said.

"We'd spend 6 months integrating. Meetings, compliance, their tech stack. We'd lose our independence. We'd become a feature in their ecosystem. And if they decide to kill the partnership? We're dead."

"But it's Google."

"Exactly. It's Google. They don't need us. We need them. That's not a partnership, that's a dependency."

He fought me. For a week. He saw the logo on our website. The credibility. The future.

I showed him the deck. The timeline. The 6-month integration that would derail our roadmap. The feature requests that weren't in our plan. The risk.

"What's the probability this helps us find PMF?" I asked.

"I don't know... 30%?"

"What's the probability it kills our focus?"

He paused. "70%."

"Then it's no."

Hard choice. Right choice.

Two months later: Google killed a similar partnership with another startup. We would have been roadkill.
